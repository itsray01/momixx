**Most silicone is never recycled.** Silicones Europe, the industry association, says "the majority of silicone waste is not recycled but instead ends up in landfills or is used for energy recovery". Silicone's durability is a strength in use. At the end of a product's life it becomes a problem, because silicone does not biodegrade.

## Where does silicone waste go today?

There are three main destinations:

- **Landfill.** Silicone is chemically stable and not biodegradable, so it persists.
- **Incineration with energy recovery.** Burning silicone produces carbon dioxide, water and amorphous silica. Because silicone does not degrade, researchers note that incineration is often preferred over landfill.
- **Recycling.** This is a small share. Chemical recycling handled an estimated 35,000 to 45,000 tonnes in 2024, against 2.9 to 3.0 million tonnes of silicone produced worldwide.

A 2026 environmental product declaration for extruded silicone profiles illustrates the default. It models end of life as **73% incineration with energy recovery and 27% landfill**, and states that the product "is not mechanically recyclable".

## Why is so little silicone recycled?

**1. It is hard to collect.** Silicone is spread across millions of small products: cables, seals, kitchenware, medical parts. It is often bonded to other materials, so clean, sorted waste streams are rare.

**2. It cannot be melted.** Cured silicone is a thermoset, so the melt-and-remould process used for plastics does not work. Grinding it into filler lowers quality. Chemical recycling restores quality but needs specialist chemistry and purification. (See [silicone recycling explained](/insights/silicone-recycling-explained).)

**3. The industry is small.** A 2024 review counts only one to two dozen small and medium-sized recyclers worldwide. It estimates the addressable feedstock at about 100,000 to 150,000 tonnes a year, much of it processed in Asia.

**4. Regulatory uncertainty.** Chemical recycling produces the cyclic siloxanes D4, D5 and D6. The EU listed them as substances of very high concern in 2018. Silicones Europe and the same review warn that stricter global controls on these substances could make recycling processes that generate them harder to run.

## What is changing?

Large silicone producers have begun investing in recycling. Elkem has piloted chemical recycling in France and launched 100%-recycled silicone grades for labels in 2025. Dow announced a silicone recycling facility in Kentucky with Circusil in 2023.

Brands are also asking for recycled content they can **prove**. Certification schemes such as GRS and ISCC PLUS audit the chain of custody from waste to finished product (see [GRS vs ISCC PLUS vs SCS](/insights/grs-vs-iscc-plus-vs-scs)).
