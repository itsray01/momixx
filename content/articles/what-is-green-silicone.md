**Green silicone is silicone made without making more waste: most often, silicone with certified recycled content.** The term has no official definition, so it is worth knowing what people mean by it, which parts can be checked, and which cannot.

## What do people mean by green silicone?

Three things, usually together:

- **Recycled content.** Silicone made partly or wholly from silicone waste, such as factory offcuts and used products, instead of new raw material. This is the meaning that can be certified, and the one this article is mostly about.
- **A smaller footprint.** Silicone made with less energy and less waste: more efficient ovens and mixers, solar power at the factory, and coating methods that release fewer solvent fumes.
- **Safer chemistry.** Standard silicone rubber contains no fluorine, so it is not a PFAS ("forever chemical"). [High-density silicone](/products/momixx-high-density) can replace fluorinated rubber (FKM) in many seals, straps and parts.

A supplier can reasonably call its silicone "green" on any of these grounds. The question for a buyer is which one is meant, and what evidence sits behind it.

## Why can recycled silicone perform like new?

Set silicone cannot be melted and reshaped the way most plastics can. So there are two ways to recycle it. Grinding it produces a powder that is mixed into new material as a filler, and the result is weaker: that is downcycling. Chemical recycling breaks the silicone back down to its basic building blocks, which are cleaned and rebuilt into new silicone. Because the chain is rebuilt from the start, the new material is comparable to silicone made from fresh raw material. [How silicone recycling works](/insights/silicone-recycling-explained) goes through both routes.

MoMixx recycles chemically, which is why our recycled silicone is described as performing like new. Very little silicone is recycled this way worldwide: researchers put chemically recycled silicone at about 35,000 to 45,000 tonnes in 2024, against roughly 3 million tonnes produced.

## How do you check a green silicone claim?

A recycled-content claim is only as good as the audit behind it. Four schemes cover silicone:

- **GRS (Global Recycled Standard)** and **RCS (Recycled Claim Standard)**, both owned by Textile Exchange. RCS verifies recycled content and the chain of custody; GRS adds social, environmental and chemical requirements. Each certified company has a scope certificate with a number that can be checked in Textile Exchange's public database.
- **ISCC PLUS**, an international scheme for recycled and circular raw materials, common in chemical and plastics supply chains. It allows mass balance, where recycled content is allocated in certified records rather than kept physically separate.
- **SCS Recycled Content Certification**, from SCS Global Services, common in North America.

For any of them, ask for the certificate number and the scheme, and check it in the scheme's database. For a specific delivery, ask for the transaction certificate that covers it; a scope certificate shows a company is certified, not that a given batch is. [GRS, ISCC PLUS and SCS compared](/insights/grs-vs-iscc-plus-vs-scs) explains the differences in detail.
