**Silicon is a chemical element. Silicone is a material made from it.** Silicon is a hard, grey solid used in computer chips. Silicone is soft and flexible, and turns up in everything from phone cables to medical tubing.

## What is silicon?

Silicon (symbol **Si**) is the second most common element in Earth's crust after oxygen. It makes up **more than a quarter of the crust by weight**. In nature it is always joined to oxygen, as quartz sand and in many rocks.

Pure silicon is hard and brittle. It is a semiconductor, meaning its ability to carry electricity can be finely controlled. That is why chips and solar cells are built on it.

## What is silicone?

Silicone is **man-made**. Its molecules are long chains of silicon and oxygen atoms, with small carbon-based groups attached. The most common type is **PDMS** (polydimethylsiloxane).

That chain makes silicone **heat-resistant**, **flexible even in the cold** and **long-lasting** in weather and sunlight. Many silicone rubbers stay bendy at temperatures that soften ordinary plastics. Depending on how the chains are built and linked, silicone can be a **liquid, gel, rubber or hard resin**.

## How does sand become silicone?

1. **Sand to silicon.** Quartz is heated with carbon in electric furnaces, which strips out the oxygen. Wacker, a major silicone maker, describes making silicon "from quartz and coal at 2,000 °C".
2. **Silicon to building blocks.** Ground silicon reacts with a gas called methyl chloride, with copper to speed things up. This "direct process" dates from the early 1940s and is still the main method.
3. **Building blocks to silicone.** These react with water and link into long chains and rings. This is raw silicone.
4. **Silicone to compound.** Raw silicone is mixed with fillers and additives into a ready-to-use **compound**, tuned for fire resistance, colour, strength or feel. This is where Momixx works.
5. **Compound to product.** The compound is extruded (pushed through a shaped opening), moulded or coated into parts, then cured so it sets.

## Why does the difference matter?

Mixing up the words sends buyers, investors and searchers to the wrong suppliers, data and rules. Silicon is a strategic raw material: the US added it to its critical-minerals list in November 2025. Silicone has its own supply chain, standards and recycling routes.
