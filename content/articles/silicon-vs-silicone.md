**Silicon is a chemical element. Silicone is a material made from it.** The two words differ by one letter, but they describe very different things. One is a hard, grey solid used in computer chips. The other is a soft, flexible polymer used in everything from phone cables to medical tubing.

## What is silicon?

Silicon (symbol **Si**, atomic number **14**) is the second most abundant element in Earth's crust after oxygen, making up about **27.7% of the crust by mass**. It never occurs on its own in nature. It is found combined with oxygen as silica (quartz sand) and silicates.

Pure silicon is hard, brittle and a semiconductor, which is why it is the base of computer chips and solar cells.

## What is silicone?

Silicone is a **man-made polymer**. Its backbone is a chain of alternating silicon and oxygen atoms (–Si–O–Si–O–), called a **siloxane** chain. Each silicon atom usually carries two organic groups, most often methyl groups. The most common silicone is **polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS)**.

That silicon–oxygen backbone is what gives silicone its useful mix of properties:

- **Heat resistance:** many silicone rubbers stay flexible well above the temperatures that soften common plastics.
- **Flexibility:** it stays soft and elastic, including in the cold.
- **Durability:** it resists weathering, UV and ageing.

Depending on chain length and how the chains are linked, silicone can be a **liquid, gel, rubber or resin**.

## How does sand become silicone?

1. **Quartz to silicon metal.** Quartz (silicon dioxide) is reduced with carbon in electric furnaces at very high temperature. Wacker describes making silicon "from quartz and coal at 2,000 °C". The chemical industry is one of silicon metal's main users, in particular for making silicones.
2. **Silicon to silane.** In the **direct process**, ground silicon reacts with methyl chloride over a copper catalyst to form a mixture of methylchlorosilanes, mainly dimethyldichlorosilane. Eugene Rochow in the US and Richard Müller in Germany developed it independently in the early 1940s, and it is still the industry's main route.
3. **Silane to silicone.** The chlorosilanes are hydrolysed and condensed into siloxane chains and rings, the base polymers of silicone.
4. **Silicone to compound.** Base polymers are blended with fillers and additives into a ready-to-use **compound**, tuned for fire retardancy, colour, strength or feel. This is where Momixx works.
5. **Compound to product.** The compound is extruded, moulded or coated into finished parts, then cured.

## Why does the difference matter?

For buyers, investors and anyone searching online, mixing up the two words leads to the wrong suppliers, the wrong market data and the wrong regulations. Silicon is tracked as a strategic raw material: the US added it to its critical-minerals list in November 2025. Silicone is a specialty material made from silicon, with its own supply chain, standards and recycling routes.
